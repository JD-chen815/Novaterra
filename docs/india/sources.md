Hawa Mahal is component evidence for jharokhas, cusped arches, brackets and pale trim only; do not copy its whole stepped facade. No Taj Mahal imagery or Agra-derived composition.

## Modeling synthesis

1. Build a contiguous 2–3-storey street wall, with one larger three-storey corner haveli. Keep ordinary shops visibly smaller than the hero building; do not fill the island with palaces.
2. Long horizontal cream bands and a flat terrace/parapet do much of the identity work. Treat domes as sparse roof pavilions, not the roof of every shop.
3. Ground-floor openings and signs follow a narrow repeating bay rhythm; make arcades genuinely shaded and deep enough for people. A reasonable invented bay is about one shop width, with posts visually slender relative to the opening.
4. The typical observed upper window/opening is taller than wide. Give jharokhas projecting polygonal bases, bracket shadows and recessed dark openings/lattice instead of painting arches onto flat boxes.
5. Use dusty terracotta, salmon rose and warm ochre with cream linework. Reserve teal, indigo, yellow and green for textiles, shutters, signage and vehicles; J1 is much less candy-pink than generic Jaipur art.
6. Roof chhatris need a visible open void: legs and arched canopy support a small dome. Their pavilion height can be roughly a floor in the miniature, but this is a stylized estimate.
7. Street life belongs at the building threshold: awnings, fabric stacks, small cups, snack case, a few scooters and sparse trees. Leave route clearance; moving actors should use an unobstructed sidewalk/road.
8. Auto-rickshaws should read as compact three-wheel vehicles with a canopy and open passenger side; use the J1 green/yellow paint combination and a dark roof/interior rather than treating them as miniature buses.

## Implemented interpretation

The authoring uses estimated miniature dimensions, nine connected district buildings, a fictional haveli and a compressed loop road. It does not reproduce Hawa Mahal or claim surveyed historic accuracy. No reference photographs are embedded in the website. The artwork, models and textures are procedural. Noto Sans Devanagari is embedded under SIL OFL 1.1, with its license redistributed in `assets/india-worlds/OFL.txt`.

The final font payload uses the official Google Fonts WOFF2 distribution (variable weight 400–700, Devanagari / Latin / Latin-ext unicode subsets), embedded as data URLs after retrieval from https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Sans+Devanagari:wght@400..700&display=block . No Google font request occurs at runtime. Both Hindi and Latin text are explicitly loaded before painting signs.
